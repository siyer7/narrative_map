* Say that each narr has its own independent on/off markov chain, one step per timestep, so any number of narrs can be on at once. A run has to last at least 4 timesteps before the narr can switch, so the story does not flicker. The script prints each narr's on and off times. Seed 0 gives narr1 4 switches, narr2 8, side narr 8, never two narrs at the same timestep; the number of narrs on at a timestep is 0, 1, 2, 3 for 3, 39, 44, 14 timesteps.
* Say that narr1 is green and narr2 purple wherever they appear: their rows in the input matrix (shade = value; side narr in greys) and their lines in the narrative continuity, narrative departure and cosine panels. Local and bayesian surprisal are blue.
* Say that every narr shares one mean feature value (1), so none reads as brighter than the others in the input matrix. While a narr is on, each of its 5 features is drawn from a gaussian over that value (sd .15), so features vary from timestep to timestep rather than sitting still; while off, its features are roughly 0 (noise, sd .05).
* Say that the three metrics are read off the input, not off which narrs are on. For a narr, prob_narr_resumed(t) = sum over its 5 features of max(input(t) - input(t-1), 0), how much its features increased since the previous timestep; prob_narr_discontinued is the same with the sign switched. An event is a stretch of a narr being on. local_surprisal(t) = prob_narr_resumed summed over all three narrs: big at every event start, i.e. any narr coming on, small elsewhere. * Say that bayesian_surprisal(t) = prob_narr_resumed summed over narr1 and narr2 only; side narr coming on does not count. At least 4.37 at narrative event starts, at most 2.00 elsewhere.
* Say that we also try reading a narr off the input by how much the input *looks like* that narr, rather than by how much it changed. Give each narr a template: the mean feature value (1) on its own 5 features, 0 on the other 10. cos(narr_template, input(t)) is then a level, not a change, so it is high all through the narr's events and low in the gaps, instead of spiking at the edges.
* Say we read the template two ways, since the normalisation is the whole question. narrative_continuity(t) = input(t) dotted with the template's unit direction: how far the input reaches along the template, dividing by nothing. cos_vs_input(t) is that same quantity divided by the norm of all 15 features, so the other narrs sit in its denominator. The projection does not normalize by feature length, which is the only difference between them: narrative_continuity = cos_vs_input x the input's norm, which the script asserts.
* Say the same thing in 2d, where it can be read by hand: let the two features be [narr1, narr2], so narr1's direction is [1, 0]. With both narrs on the input is [1, 1], whose length is pythagoras, sqrt(2). The projection is 1 either way, since the direction is 0 on narr2's feature and that term drops out of the dot product; the cosine falls from 1 to 1/sqrt(2) = .71, because narr2's feature is in its denominator. The threshold is needed because a plain diff < 0 fires at 49 of 99 timesteps, the diff of wobble being negative half the time. At -1 it fires exactly 2 times for narr1 and 4 for narr2, catching every event end with no false alarms, and -1 sits in a wide gap (ends near -2, background topping out at -0.55).
* Say the departures are what would be sent to the hippocampus, as the signal to commit that narrative to memory. Sustained off is the quiet case, not the risky one: while a narr is off its features are noise at sd .05, so continuity_diff has sd .051 and dips at worst to -0.06 across narr2's longest 21-timestep gap. While a narr is on, its features wobble at sd .15, so continuity_diff has sd .21 and dips to -0.55 — still inside the threshold, but that is where the margin is thin.
* Say both tell on from off, projection more cleanly: narr1 is at least 1.83 while on and at most 0.06 while off; cos_vs_input is at least .51 on and at most .28 off. For narr2 the cosine nearly overlaps, .53 on vs .49 off.

## Notes

* local surprisal says an event started, but not in which narr. Bayesian surprisal keeps only the narratives' event starts; each narr discontinuity marks the ends of its own narrative's events. So an event start shows up in local surprisal always, and in bayesian surprisal unless it is side narr's.
* the sanity check — a timestep where narr_x's features are ~0 should score low — passes for both (off mean .00 and -.01 for projection, .02 and .01 for cos_vs_input), since both keep the magnitude that says the narr is absent. Only their denominators differ.
* the gap between event starts and the rest is narrowest for local surprisal (2.47 vs 4.43), because it sums the wobble of every feature that is on, up to 15, while the other two sum 10 or 5.
